Mira

A social outfit diary for sharing fit checks, that learns your style as you go

in progressux/uimobilesocialfashionai
designs →code →
01

the why

why i'm building it

People already take outfit pics and send them to friends, but they get buried in group chats and camera rolls. Nobody can scroll back and see what they wore last month, and the outfits we loved get forgotten.

Mira gives those photos a home. Every outfit you post goes into your own outfit diary and shows up in the feed for whoever your privacy settings allow. Over time, all those posts add up to a style history that Mira can learn from, so it can help you figure out what to wear. The name comes from mirror, and Mira is also the name of the in-app style assistant.

It's for people 16 and up who love fashion, already take outfit photos, and like sharing them with friends.

02

how it works

the plan

Mira comes in two layers. The diary and the feed come first, because that's what makes people open the app every day. The learning layer grows on top of what they post.

1. The diary

  • Post your OOTD (outfit of the day): take or upload a photo, add up to three status tags for how you're feeling ("feeling: happy"), write a description, and rate the look on style, comfort and confidence. Only one rating is required, so posting takes seconds. Post as many outfits a day as you like.
  • Your records: every outfit is saved to a month calendar, with your streak and outfit count underneath. On days with more than one post, the calendar shows one of them at random.

2. The social part

  • Feed: two sections, friends and explore. Friends shows fit checks from people you've added, and explore shows outfits from people you don't know yet. Every post is full height, with likes, comments, saves and shares.
  • Adding friends: add people by username or invite.
  • Share more than outfits: wishlist items and fun stats, like your most-worn piece or your favorite style this month.

3. The learning part

  • Wardrobe: Mira picks the pieces out of your outfit photos and builds your closet for you. Each piece links to every outfit it shows up in.
  • Insights: see what you reach for, what you never wear, and which combinations you rate highest.
  • Mira, the assistant: ask "what should I wear today?" or "what have I worn with these jeans?" and get answers based on your own outfits instead of trends.

The app is built in React with Supabase for the database, auth and photo storage. The assistant runs on a separate Python service (mira-rag) that splits each outfit photo into garments, embeds them with FashionCLIP and answers questions with the outfits it used as sources. The app talks to it through two endpoints, one to ingest a new outfit and one to ask a question, so the models can change without touching the app.

Key decisions

  • Social first, smart later: the diary and feed are useful from the first post. The learning features need weeks of outfits to be any good, so they come after people are already posting.
  • Your posts are the data: nobody has to catalog their whole closet. Mira learns from fit checks people were going to post anyway.
  • AI does the data entry: Mira suggests tags and categories and the user confirms, so the app never feels like homework.
  • You choose who sees what: privacy settings on your account control who can see your posts, and blocking, reporting and deleting your data are easy.
  • Don't judge anyone's style: Mira helps you understand your style, not change it to match a trend.
  • Soft and playful look: cream, black and sand with blush, lilac and powder-blue accents, a pixel display font, and springy motion like buttons that squish and doodles that draw themselves in.
03

where it's at

current state

The product spec, screen inventory and design rules are written, and the first three screens are drafted in Figma from a small component set (header nav, post card, status tags and a star-shaped add button):

  • Records: your outfit diary as a month calendar, with your streak and outfit count.
  • Feed: full-height fit checks in two sections, friends and explore, tagged with how the wearer felt.
  • Profile: profile, account and privacy settings on one page.

The Figma file is organized into cover, read me, components, screens, playground and archive pages so it's ready to grow.

04

what's next

the to-do list

The diary and social screens come first, then the learning features:

  • Foundations: color, type and spacing tokens, plus buttons, inputs, star ratings, chips, empty states and bottom sheets.
  • Onboarding: welcome, sign up, log in, photo and notification permissions, then "post today's fit."
  • Post an outfit: the star button's flow: camera or upload, status tags, description, ratings, then post.
  • Friends and explore: add by username or invite, the explore section, accept requests, comments sheet, saved posts, block and report.
  • Diary views: outfit detail, edit and delete, week and year calendar views, public profiles.
  • Notifications: a gentle daily reminder and friend activity, each with its own toggle.
  • Learning features: wardrobe built from your photos, insights, outfit planning and the Mira assistant.
  • Build it: React app on Supabase, then the mira-rag service once 30 real outfits are logged.
